I don't really like April Fools' Day.

Most of the jokes are obvious and the rest are companies pretending they've invented a product nobody asked for.

This morning somebody posted that ZeroDays had finally recruited a real hacker.

I nearly believed it for a second.

Fair play, that was a good one.

Apparently a few of their members didn't find it funny. One of them sent me a message saying I should be careful what I say about people online.

I sent back, "You should be careful where you send messages from."

He deleted it immediately.

Too late, obviously.

By that point I had already mirrored the entire exchange across my private evidence network. Deleting a message after it reaches me is like trying to put smoke back into a candle.

That's probably why he went quiet.

ZeroDays keep acting like this is some sort of rivalry. It isn't. A rivalry requires both sides to be at roughly the same level. I don't have a rivalry with the toaster either, even though it has burned my breakfast twice this week.

Actually, the toaster has caused me more problems.

I spent part of the afternoon clearing out a cupboard. Found a packet of biscuits that expired last year and a cable for something I don't remember owning.

Kept the cable.

You never throw away an unknown cable. It might be the exact one you need six years from now, and then who looks stupid?

Not me.

The biscuits went in the bin, though. I have limits.
